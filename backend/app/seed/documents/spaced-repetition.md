# Why spaced repetition works

Forgetting is not a bug in memory, it is how memory decides what matters. Every time you successfully recall something just before you would have forgotten it, the memory becomes more durable and the next review can wait longer.

## The forgetting curve

Without review, most of what we read is gone within a week. The curve is steep at first and then flattens: the first few days do most of the damage. A single review a day after reading changes the shape of the curve dramatically.

## Effort is the point

Rereading feels productive because the material is familiar, but familiarity is not recall. Retrieval practice works precisely because it is a little uncomfortable. If a review feels effortless, the interval was too short.

## Making good cards

- One idea per card. If the answer has a list in it, split the card.
- Write the question so that only one answer is correct.
- Add context from where you found it: a card without a source is hard to trust a year later.
- Delete cards you keep failing and do not care about. The deck should serve you, not the other way around.

## From highlights to cards

The bridge between reading and remembering is the note you write next to a highlight. A highlight says "this looked important". A note says what it means. A card asks you to prove you still know it.
